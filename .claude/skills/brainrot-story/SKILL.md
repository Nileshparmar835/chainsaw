# Build a joke with consequences

Take the topic, language, desired runtime and any returning cast from the brief. Draft three different opening images and opening lines in scratch. Pick the one a viewer can understand with the sound off, that poses a specific question with a cost. Write a concrete payoff before expanding the middle.

| Topic clue | Story choice | A visual proof of the twist |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary object with a grievance | Give it one legitimate complaint and ridiculous authority | The receipt, rule or contract from the opening changes who wins |
| Choice, price, wish or trap | Establish one visible rule; let the hero exploit it, then pay for it | A detail planted before the choice becomes the trap |
| Body or science question | Stage the mechanism as a small workplace, then return outside | The physical outcome agrees with the actual mechanism; check the core fact against a reliable source |
| Chase, countdown or challenge | Give the lead a physical goal and give the rival a faster solution | The timer and object visibly move; the win changes the rule |
| Familiar internet gag | Reverse one assumption about who has agency | A recognizable silhouette acts before anyone explains it |

Use four to eight purposeful scenes. Alternate orientation shots with close reactions and prop inserts. Escalate through changes of information, status or physical cause. Keep one plot and one reveal legible on a phone. Deliver the payoff on screen; let the final consequence lead visually into the opening if that makes the joke sharper. Cut greetings, narrator explanations of visible gags and generic demands to keep watching.

Choose the smallest cast that can perform the premise. Give each returning character a silhouette, color, motive, voice and signature gesture. Allow a prop to be the lead. Separate speaking roles from on-screen cameos. In episode.json every beat has one main visual action, one speaker, a short playable line, optional performance marks and a motivated sound. Longer sentences can span short consecutive beats. Give each reaction enough time to register at actual audio speed. Read the script aloud; remove a clause if physical action can deliver it.

Example: "A vending machine demands a tip" can open with a soda dangling over the slot as a tip screen rises. A tip of one cent buys one drop. The final joke should cash out that exact rule. Invent the remaining staging and ending for each new brief.
